# Coastal Foraging Reference Documents

> **Biotoxin closures are state-monitored — if the hotline is unreachable, treat May–October mussels as quarantined by default. Unsafe toxin levels sometimes occur outside the quarantine too (CDPH p.3): clear water and a winter month are not an all-clear.**

California Biotoxin Hotline: 1-800-553-4133 (CA Dept of Public Health, Marine Biotoxin Monitoring Program)

## Documents

### cdph_marine_biotoxins.pdf
**Title:** Natural Marine Toxins: PSP and Domoic Acid
**Source:** California Dept of Public Health (CDPH), Environmental Health Division
**Date:** 2014 (imprint "UCSGEP 14-1W April 8, 2014", p.5)

Covers both major California marine toxin threats: paralytic shellfish poisoning (PSP) and domoic acid (amnesic shellfish poisoning, ASP). Explains toxin sources (phytoplankton), accumulation in filter feeders, symptoms, onset times, and the CDPH monitoring program. PSP toxins and domoic acid are not destroyed by cooking (CDPH p.1: "Cooking does not destroy the toxins") — no safe home test exists. Unsafe domoic acid levels have been found in mussels, oysters, razor clams (meat and viscera), crab (viscera and meat), lobster (viscera), and sardines and anchovies (CDPH p.1).

### cdfw_ocean_sport_fishing_regs_2025.pdf
**Title:** 2025 California Ocean Sport Fishing Regulations (Last Updated September 26, 2025)
**Source:** California Dept of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW)
**Date:** 2025

Official take limits, open seasons, size minimums, gear restrictions, and license requirements for California ocean sport fishing including shellfish (mussels, clams, abalone, urchin), finfish (rockfish, salmon, halibut), and crustaceans (dungeness crab, rock crab). Verify against current year at: https://wildlife.ca.gov/Regulations/Sport-Fishing/Ocean

### shellfish_id_guide_wa_dnr.pdf
**Title:** Washington Sport Fishing Rules: Shellfish/Seaweed Rules (Effective July 1, 2016 – June 30, 2017)
**Source:** Washington Dept of Fish and Wildlife (WDFW), .gov
**Date:** 2016–2017

Washington state shellfish and seaweed harvesting rules — species identification, daily limits, size limits, and closed areas for clams, oysters, mussels, crab, and sea urchin. Pacific Northwest species overlap significantly with Northern California; useful for species ID features and harvest method guidance. Check current WA rules at: https://wdfw.wa.gov/fishing/shellfishing-regulations

## Safety Notes

- PSP and domoic acid toxins are **not destroyed by cooking**. No visual, smell, or taste test detects them.
- California closes harvesting based on routine water sampling — check before every harvest:
  https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DRSEM/Pages/smb/default.aspx
- Dungeness and other crabs: domoic acid can reach unsafe levels in the meat as well as the viscera (CDPH p.1, "crab (viscera and meat)"). During a domoic acid advisory do not eat crab at all; at other times remove and discard the viscera right after capture (CDPH p.4) and eat the meat.
- Red abalone: **closed to sport fishing statewide** since 2018 (do not harvest).
- Sea palm (*Postelsia palmaeformis*): recreational harvest **prohibited in California**: it may not be cut or disturbed, and take or possession is prohibited (CDFW 2025 regulations, T14 CCR §30.10, p.44 and the table on p.54). No Oregon source is on the shelf.
- Quarantine rule when hotline is unreachable: treat all mussels harvested May–October as unsafe. Outside those months unsafe levels still sometimes occur (CDPH p.3).
