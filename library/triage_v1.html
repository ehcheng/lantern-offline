Severe bleeding / amputation

NOW: direct, firm pressure first — Where There Is No Doctor, "bleeding" (PDF). Limb bleeding that will not stop: tourniquet 2–3 inches above the wound, or high-and-tight if you cannot see where it is bleeding from — TCCC Guidelines 2024 (PDF), the current standard for hemorrhage control. Nosebleed: pinch the nose firmly just below the hard, bony part and hold for 10 minutes without letting go to check; still bleeding, pinch 10 minutes more. Where There Is No Doctor, "nosebleeds" (opens from a drive).
DEEPER: FM 4-25.11 First Aid (2002, background) · TCCC CPG folder (High Bilateral Amputations, Vascular Injury, Damage Control Resuscitation) · Emergency War Surgery (2004, background; last resort, trained hands).

Unconscious / not breathing

NOW: check response, open airway, start CPR if no normal breathing. A baby just born who is not breathing: see childbirth (newborn resuscitation), not adult CPR. WikiMed: "Cardiopulmonary resuscitation" for current compression rates. Where There Is No Doctor, "breathing" (PDF) for rescue breathing.
DEEPER: TCCC Airway Management · Where There Is No Doctor, "loss of consciousness" (PDF).

Choking

NOW: cough, then back blows, then abdominal thrusts (Heimlich); infants: back blows + chest thrusts. WikiMed: "Choking". Also Where There Is No Doctor, first aid chapter (PDF).

Chest pain / trouble breathing

NOW: sit them up, loosen clothing. Crushing chest pain, pain spreading to arm or jaw, sweating, short of breath: Where There Is No Doctor, "heart attack" (PDF, page 28; opens from a drive). A person who is awake and talking with chest pain needs to sit and rest, not chest compressions; CPR is only for someone who does not respond and is not breathing normally (unconscious). WikiMed "Myocardial infarction". Penetrating chest wound: seal it — TCCC Thoracic Injury.
DEEPER: MSF Clinical Guidelines (respiratory chapters) · Where There Is No Doctor.

Stroke: face droops, one arm weak, speech slurred

NOW: check three things. Ask them to smile: one side of the face droops. Ask them to raise both arms: one drifts down or is weak. Ask them to repeat a simple sentence: the speech is slurred or will not come. Any one of these is a stroke until proven otherwise; get them to a hospital as fast as you can (hospitals can treat a stroke best within the first 3 hours). Where There Is No Doctor, "A stroke is an emergency!" (opens from a drive).
DEEPER: WikiMed "Stroke" (needs the server). Sudden confusion with fever and a stiff neck is not a stroke: see fever.

Shock — pale, cold, sweaty, fast weak pulse

NOW: lay flat, stop any bleeding, keep warm. Where There Is No Doctor, "shock" (PDF).
DEEPER: TCCC Damage Control Resuscitation · TCCC Hypothermia Prevention (shock casualties chill fast).

Burns

NOW: minor burn: right away, pour clean cool water (not ice) over the burned skin for 15 to 30 minutes — Where There Is No Doctor, burns (PDF, page 38; opens from a drive). Dangerous burn (deep, larger than 10 of the person's palms, on the face, a joint or the genitals, with other injuries, or on a child): get help; on the way, cover it with a very clean cloth and do not soak it in cold water, which can chill the person dangerously (same PDF, page 38). Blisters: if a blister has not opened, do not try to pop it; opened blisters get infected more easily (same PDF, page 39). Never put ice, grease or fat on a burn — Where There Is No Doctor, "burns" (HealthWiki page; opens from a drive). Cover with loose, dry, clean gauze or sheet — TCCC Burn Care.
DEEPER: MSF Clinical Guidelines burns chapter · Where There Is No Doctor, burns (PDF) (fluid needs, infection watch).

Kitchen / grease fire — a pan of oil or fat on fire

NOW: "DO NOT put water on a fire caused by cooking grease. This will make the fire spread! Grease fires are put out by stopping them from getting air. To put the fire out, cover it with a blanket or heavy cloth, or throw sand or dirt on the grease." Turn the heat off if you can reach it; leave the cover on until everything is cold. — Hesperian, A Community Guide to Environmental Health, "Fire Safety" (opens from a drive). Flames spreading beyond the pan: everyone out, close the door behind you. Anyone burned: burns.
DEEPER: Fire Safety (preventing house fires: enclosed cooking fires, fuels stored outside, a covered bucket of sand by the stove) · Ready.gov, home fires (smoke alarms, escape plan) · wildfire: fire safety shelf.

Broken bone / sprain / dislocation

NOW: immobilize as it lies, splint joints above and below. Where There Is No Doctor, broken bones, dislocations and sprains (PDF).
DEEPER: Where There Is No Doctor · TCCC Compartment Syndrome (tight, worsening pain after splint = emergency).

Wounds & wound infection

NOW: irrigate hard with clean water, do NOT suture dirty wounds. Where There Is No Doctor, "wounds" (PDF; opens from a drive) · medics: TCCC Wound Management.
DEEPER: TCCC Debridement · ICRC First Aid in Armed Conflict (2006, background) · red streaks / spreading heat / fever = infection: MSF Clinical Guidelines.

Head injury

NOW: Where There Is No Doctor, "head injuries" (opens from a drive): after a blow to the head, watch the person for 24 hours; if they fall asleep, wake them every few hours to check they still answer and think clearly. Also check for a back or neck injury. Worsening drowsiness, unequal pupils, repeated vomiting = critical. Medics: TCCC Traumatic Brain Injury. Nosebleed: see bleeding.
DEEPER: WikiMed "Concussion", "Traumatic brain injury".

Seizure / convulsions: shaking, falls, will not respond

NOW: clear the space around them so they do not get hurt. Turn them on their side so they do not choke if they vomit. Do not hold them down, and do not put anything in the mouth or between the teeth. Get medical help; a seizure that lasts more than 5 minutes is an emergency. Afterwards: a seizure with fever and a stiff neck can be meningitis, go to a hospital now (fever); after diarrhea, give fluids (dehydration); after a head blow, see head injury. Where There Is No Doctor, "seizures, convulsions" · Hesperian, Epilepsy and Seizures, "First aid for seizures" (both open from a drive).
DEEPER: Epilepsy and Seizures, "when seizures are an emergency" · WHO mhGAP Intervention Guide (epilepsy and seizures, for non-specialists). Medicines and doses: the printed drug guide only.

Diabetic: low blood sugar (confused, sweating, shaking, passing out)

NOW: a person with diabetes who is confused, sweating, shaking or acting drunk may have low blood sugar. Awake and able to swallow: give sugar by mouth right away (fruit juice, soda, candy, or sugar stirred into water), then a full meal soon after. Not awake: never give food or drink by mouth; the book says to put a pinch of sugar or honey under the tongue instead, a little at a time. Still confused 15 minutes after the sugar, or not waking up: get medical help now. Not sure whether the sugar is low or high: treat it as low (give a little sugar) and get medical help. Do not give insulin unless you are certain the sugar is high: insulin given for low blood sugar can kill. Not breathing: see unconscious. Where There Is No Doctor, "diabetic emergencies" · "loss of consciousness" ("Never give anything by mouth to a person who is unconscious"; both open from a drive).
DEEPER: Where There Is No Doctor, high blood sugar (hyperglycemia) · Where There Is No Doctor, chapter 21: diabetes. Medicines and doses: the printed drug guide only.

Poisoning — pills or medicine, overdose, chemical, plant, mushroom

NOW: find out what, how much, and when; keep the bottle, packet or a piece of the plant. A child who swallowed pills, an adult who took too much medicine, bleach or cleaner, fuel: if any phone works, call poison control first (US: 1-800-222-1222, 24 hours). Do NOT make them vomit after chemicals, petroleum (fuel, kerosene), or if they are drowsy or having trouble breathing; never give salt water to cause vomiting (it can kill a child). Where There Is No Doctor, "poison" (PDF; opens from a drive). Current guidance differs from that page: do not make the person vomit (the page suggests a finger in the throat or swallowing salt; current first aid advises against both). Mushrooms/plants: compare against plant_id photo folders (death cap, destroying angel, fly agaric, false morel, nightshades, death camas, foxglove, false hellebore, water hemlock, giant hogweed…). WikiMed: "Mushroom poisoning", or the specific plant name.
DEEPER: MSF Clinical Guidelines · Where There Is No Doctor, "poison" (PDF) · WikiMed "Drug overdose". Amanita (death cap) suspicion: symptoms improving after 12–24 h does NOT mean safe — liver failure follows. Treat as critical. Species card: death cap (from a drive: README.txt).

Whole group with headache, nausea, confusion — carbon monoxide

NOW: get everyone into fresh air immediately; kill stoves/generators/heaters. WikiMed: "Carbon monoxide poisoning". Suspect CO whenever several people sicken together indoors with combustion running. Generators, camp stoves and grills only outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows: Ready.gov, power outages. Signs and prevention: Hesperian, "Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning" (opens from a drive).

Snakebite / animal bite

NOW: snake: keep the bitten limb below the heart and still (splint or sling), NO cutting, NO sucking, NO ice, NO tourniquet — Where There Is No Doctor, "stings and bites". North American rattlesnakes and most other American pit vipers (copperhead, cottonmouth): do not wrap. That page's "wrap the bitten area tightly" list is for coral snakes, mambas, sea snakes, most cobras and South American rattlesnakes; for most vipers it says "do not wrap the bite". WikiMed: "Snakebite". Animal bite (dog, cat, rat, bat): wash hard with soap and running water, leave open, watch for infection — Where There Is No Doctor, "medicines for animal bites" (when a bite needs rabies or tetanus treatment; opens from a drive). Rabies risk: WikiMed "Rabies".
DEEPER: Where There Is No Doctor, stings and bites (PDF).

Severe allergic reaction — swelling face/throat, hives, wheeze

NOW: epinephrine auto-injector if available, thigh, immediately. WikiMed: "Anaphylaxis" (needs the server). From a drive: Where There Is No Doctor, "allergy: mild or severe (anaphylaxis)" (signs of a severe allergy, where to inject). Sting or bite followed by swelling of the lips, face or throat, or trouble breathing: this card, now.
DEEPER: MSF Clinical Guidelines.

Fever / suspected infection

NOW: stiff neck + fever = act fast: it can be meningitis, get medical help now — MSF Clinical Guidelines, "Bacterial meningitis" (PDF page 219): fever, severe headache, light hurts the eyes, stiff neck. Fever after a wound: also act fast (wounds). Everything else: MSF Clinical Guidelines — symptom-organized, current, made for low-resource settings. Child under 5: see sick child. To bring a fever down without medicine: cool wet cloths or a cool (but not cold) bath, and plenty of fluids to prevent dehydration (Where There Is No Doctor, "Fever"); most importantly, find and treat the cause. Fever with a rash of blisters or sores: Hesperian, "Mpox". Dengue: use paracetamol (acetaminophen) for fever and pain, never ibuprofen or aspirin. Some older pages on this box say ibuprofen; current guidance differs (New Where There Is No Doctor ch.22 p.10; MSF Clinical Guidelines p.37).
DEEPER: Where There Is No Doctor · stiff neck + fever, or fever after wound = act fast (WikiMed "Meningitis", "Sepsis", "Tetanus").

Diarrhea / vomiting / dehydration

NOW: oral rehydration — recipe and signs of dehydration in Where There Is No Doctor, belly pain and diarrhea. Keep giving fluids in small sips even if vomiting.
DEEPER: MSF Clinical Guidelines · blood in stool, or child floppy/sunken eyes = danger signs, WHO IMCI chart booklet. Prevent next case: water safety.

Severe belly pain

NOW: rigid belly, rebound pain, pain migrating to lower right, no stool+vomiting = surgical emergency. Where There Is No Doctor, pain in the belly or gut (danger signs, what you can do); WikiMed "Appendicitis".
DEEPER: MSF Clinical Guidelines · WHO Surgical Care at the District Hospital (2003, technique background) acute abdomen section. Give nothing by mouth while surgical belly suspected.

Hypothermia / frostbite

NOW: insulate from the ground, heat on the torso and armpits (not limbs first) — TCCC Hypothermia Prevention · Where There Is No Doctor, "cold emergencies" (hypothermia and frostbite; opens from a drive). Handle gently — WikiMed "Hypothermia". Frostbite: do not rub, do not thaw if refreezing is possible — WikiMed "Frostbite".
DEEPER: WikiMed "Hypothermia", "Frostbite".

Heat exhaustion / heatstroke

NOW: confused = heatstroke, whether or not they are sweating = cool aggressively NOW (water, fanning, wet cloths, immersion if possible). Where There Is No Doctor, "heat emergencies" (heat exhaustion and heat stroke; opens from a drive). WikiMed: "Heat stroke" (needs the server). Sweating, weak, very thirsty, headache, but thinking clearly = heat exhaustion: "Rest in a cool place. Take off extra clothes." Give plenty of cool drinks. "If not treated, heat exhaustion can worsen into heat stroke."

Childbirth / bleeding in pregnancy

NOW: MSF Obstetrics & Newborn Care — current, step-by-step, covers complications (cord, breech, bleeding after birth). Bleeding while pregnant, before labor: light bleeding in the first 3 months can be normal; bleeding with pain, bleeding heavier than a normal period, or any bleeding after the third month means mother and baby are in danger: go to a hospital now. Where Women Have No Doctor, "Risks and danger signs during pregnancy", bleeding from the vagina (opens from a drive). Newborn not breathing or not crying: dry the baby, rub the back and the soles of the feet, clear the mouth and nose; still not breathing after that, bag-mask breaths from the first minute — MSF Obstetrics, section 10.2 Neonatal resuscitation (PDF page 217). Not adult CPR. Placenta not out 30 to 45 minutes after the birth, or heavy bleeding before it comes: MSF Obstetrics, section 9.2 Manual removal of the placenta (PDF page 190); A Book for Midwives, the birth of the placenta. Heavy bleeding after the placenta is out: a big gush or an ongoing flow of blood is an emergency. Help her urinate and rub the womb hard and deep until it feels like a hard, round ball below the belly button; if the bleeding continues, rub harder, squeezing the womb between two hands. Get help. Where There Is No Doctor, "After the Birth", rub the womb (opens from a drive) · MSF Obstetrics, section 8.2 Early postpartum haemorrhage (PDF page 175).
DEEPER: Hesperian, A Book for Midwives (opens from a drive; all Hesperian books: Hesperian HealthWiki mirror) · WHO Pocket Book newborn sections.

Sick baby or child

NOW: WHO IMCI Chart Booklet — designed exactly for this: check danger signs, classify, act. Sleepy or hard to wake, will not drink or breastfeed, or a seizure: danger signs, act now.
DEEPER: WHO Pocket Book of Hospital Care for Children · Where There Is No Doctor, caring for children (PDF). Doses for children: printed licensed reference only.

Toothache / dental

NOW: Hesperian Where There Is No Dentist (also in the HealthWiki mirror). Swelling of the face that keeps spreading = infection reaching the bone, act now (dentist book, printed page 47; PDF page 55). Swelling of the neck, trouble swallowing or breathing = life-threatening, airway at risk: WikiMed "Ludwig's angina".

Eye injury

NOW: chemical: keep pouring clean water gently over the eye for at least 15 to 30 minutes (Where There Is No Doctor, "Injuries to the eye from chemicals"). Object embedded: do NOT pull, shield both eyes. WikiMed: "Eye injury". Where There Is No Doctor, eyes (PDF).

Panic / despair / mental crisis

NOW: WHO Psychological First Aid — listen, calm, cover basic needs, don't force talk. Someone talking about ending their life: ask them directly ("Are you having thoughts of ending your life?") and get others to help — Where There Is No Doctor, "mental health emergency" (opens from a drive). Do not leave them alone, and take away what they could use to hurt themselves (WHO mhGAP, PDF page 144).
DEEPER: WHO mhGAP Intervention Guide (depression, psychosis, seizures, self-harm — for non-specialists; self-harm and suicide: PDF page 139).

Is this water safe? (prevent the next patient)

NOW: when in doubt: boil (rolling boil 1 min). Water Treatment Library (needs the server) for field treatment methods. No tap water: the water heater tank and the toilet tank (not the bowl) hold safe drinking water; the toilet tank only if no cleaning tablet or chemical has been put in the tank; the EPA sheet on the Water shelf says not to drink flush-tank water, so treat it first if you can. Ready.gov flyer ("Water from your toilet tank (not bowl) and water heater is also safe") · EPA, Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water, PDF page 2 ("You should not use water from toilet flush tanks or bowls") · FEMA P-530, PDF page 32 ("Water heaters are also a source of emergency water").
DEEPER: WHO Drinking Water Quality · docs/water/ · sanitation to stop fecal-oral spread: docs/sanitation/. Latrines at least 30 m from any well or water source (Sphere Handbook 2018, PDF page 140, WEDC excreta disposal field manual).
